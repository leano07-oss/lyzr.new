02The problem

Seven platforms, 60 hotels, and no single place to work.

Reviews, comments, approvals and reports were all handled by hand across disconnected tools, so the agency could only grow by adding headcount.

  1. Seven platforms, one property at a timeGoogle, TripAdvisor, Expedia, Booking.com, Yelp, Facebook and Instagram were each checked separately, for every property. Some clients run 30 hotels.
  2. Every reply started from a blank pageDrafts came from AI prompts and brand guidelines lived in spreadsheets. Every review and comment gets a reply, positive ones included.
  3. Approvals over email and callsNegative and sensitive reviews needed the hotel’s sign-off, with no single place to track them. One crisis reply posted without approval is a real risk.
  4. Reports rebuilt every monthEach client report meant manual data pulls, screenshots and several AI prompts across Right Response AI and the Meta inbox.
03Native limits

A generic chatbot could not speak for 60 hotels.

Off-the-shelf tools could draft a reply. To be trusted with a hotel’s name, the platform had to pass four tests.

  1. One queue across every platformReviews from Google, Meta, TripAdvisor, Expedia and Booking.com all had to land in one place, each through the connection that platform supports.
  2. Each hotel’s own voice and factsReplies had to draw only on that property’s approved pages and documents, with no open web, so similarly named hotels never get confused.
  3. Serious complaints wait for the hotelReviews about food poisoning, injuries, lawsuits or similar issues had to be flagged straight away, and no reply could go out until the hotel approved it.
  4. Strict client separationEach hotel sees only its own properties, and a hotel group sees all of its hotels and nothing more.
04What Lyzr built

Six agents, one queue, a human on every sensitive reply.

Lyzr built the AI layer behind the agency’s own review platform: agents that triage every incoming review and message, draft replies in each brand’s voice and write the monthly client reports.

Core Capabilities

  • Triage and categorisationScores every review and comment for urgency, sentiment and themes, then sorts it into Urgent, Priority or Positive
  • Reply draftingDrafts replies to reviews, comments and DMs from each property’s policies, knowledge and approved links
  • Crisis detectionFlags safety incidents, legal threats and discrimination, and holds them for the hotel’s sign-off
  • Source checkShows the specialist exactly which policies, documents and links each draft drew on
  • Reporting insightsWrites the summaries in each client’s reputation, engagement and executive reports

06Controls

No sensitive reply goes out without the hotel.

If a review is risky, a person decides.

Client sign-off on sensitive replies
Negative and crisis-flagged replies need the hotel’s approval, and flagged items can never be posted directly.
No silent posting
Approvals the hotel has not answered in 48 hours go back to the specialist instead of posting. Crisis items never release on their own.
Confirmed publishing
An item is marked done only when Google or Meta confirms the reply, and a reply the hotel already posted on Google is never overwritten.
Approved sources only
Drafts draw only on each property’s policies, documents and approved links, and the specialist can see every source.
Strict client isolation
Hotels see only their own properties; groups see all of their hotels and nothing else.
Full audit trail
Every draft, edit, approval, escalation and post is logged with the user and timestamp.
07Re-imagined workflow

From seven logins to one prioritised queue.

Before, specialists checked each platform, drafted with AI prompts and chased approvals by email. After, every review and message lands in one queue already triaged and drafted, and negative replies go to the hotel through its own portal.
