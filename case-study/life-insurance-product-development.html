02The problem

Every launch meant days of manual research and retyped figures.

Every new product runs through an 11-step cycle of about 167 days. Three of those steps, market research, regulator filings and the policy manual, were done manually, and every retyped figure was a chance for error.

  1. Manual market researchAnalysts gathered competitor brochures, product pages and Facebook posts one insurer at a time, which took days for each new product.
  2. Figures retyped and driftingNumbers were copied into Excel comparisons and reports by hand, so they drifted from the source and were hard to check.
  3. Filings copied field by fieldThe regulator’s Key Summary and Business Plan Template repeat figures from the fact sheet. One copying error meant rework with the regulator.
  4. Manuals from scratch, approvals by emailThere was no policy manual template, and sign-offs ran over email with no record of who approved what.
03Native limits

Regulatory filings leave no room for a guessed number.

To be trusted with regulatory filings, the assistant had to pass four tests at once.

  1. Thai firstQuestions, answers and documents are in Thai, with insurance vocabulary, colloquial spellings and Thai names for each insurer.
  2. Exact numbersA payment term or a maturity percentage cannot be paraphrased. Every figure had to come from a source the reviewer could open.
  3. The regulator’s formatsThe OIC Key Summary and Business Plan Template have fixed structures, so drafts had to come out in those templates, ready to file.
  4. Inside the insurer’s boundarySign-in with the insurer’s Microsoft accounts, data and models in its own Google Cloud, and a person approving every deliverable.
04What Lyzr built

One assistant that researches, drafts and files, with people in charge.

Lyzr and partner Beryl8 delivered the Product Development Assistant on Lyzr Agent Studio and Lyzr RAG, running inside the insurer’s own Google Cloud with models served from its Vertex AI.

Core Capabilities

  • Competitor intelligence chatAnswers Thai questions about the insurer’s and competitors’ products, market statistics, the web or uploaded files in 8 to 50 seconds, with numbered citations
  • New product creationTurns a short brief into a shortlist of comparable products, a benchmark table, an Excel workbook and an insight report
  • OIC Key Summary and BPTFills the regulator’s templates from the product fact sheet, with every AI-filled field marked with its source
  • Policy manualDrafts a branded customer manual from the fact sheet and the approved filing, then checks it for coverage and consistency
  • Review consoleLets reviewers approve, reject or comment on each stage, and admins manage knowledge bases, data sources, roles and models

06Controls

Every number is checked before anyone sees it.

Agents write the SQL and draft the text. Code checks every figure against the source.

Read-only data access
Agent-written SQL runs in a read-only transaction, against an allow-list of tables, with a timeout and a row cap.
Every number checked
Each answer and draft is compared with its evidence, and a filled-in value counts only if its quote appears word for word in the source.
Sources on everything
Chat answers cite numbered sources, and filings mark which fields came from the fact sheet and which from AI.
People approve every stage
A stage under review is locked, and each decision is recorded with the reviewer’s remarks.
Corporate sign-in and roles
Single sign-on with the insurer’s Microsoft accounts, with four roles that a SuperAdmin controls.
Data stays in the insurer’s cloud
The catalog, uploads, documents and history stay in the insurer’s Google Cloud on private networking, with an append-only audit log.
07Re-imagined workflow

From browser tabs and retyped figures to one approved product case.

Before, an analyst researched insurer by insurer and retyped the same figures into each document. After, a brief starts a product case, agents draft each deliverable from the source data, and a reviewer approves each stage before the next one starts.
